# A palace between universes

The visual identity combines aged limestone, muted cream marble, dark joinery, oxidized brass, and restrained blue-green portal light. The palace is an enclosed circular hall with arcades, a ribbed dome, paired door leaves, fluted piers, ceremonial stairs, a tiled floor and a monumental mechanical gate. The raised outer stairs are architectural scenery outside the playable arena.

The library keeps the original five hiding anchors. Scanned clock, bust and chandelier assets supply recognizable detail; wood shelves, instanced leather-colored books, a librarian’s writing desk, manuscripts, carpet, stone alcoves and cool windows frame the clear central route. The displaced brass volume identifies the bookshelf mechanism without revealing the server’s selected anchor.

Color, OpenGL normal and roughness maps provide material variation. Brass has deliberately imperfect roughness rather than mirror polish. Local HDR illumination, cool hemisphere fill, warm practical sources and one bounded shadow-casting directional light provide depth. ACES filmic tone mapping and restrained exposure keep portal light from dominating. Dust, slow gate mechanisms and flickering candle geometry add small motions. This implementation uses global distance fog, not true volumetric lighting.

Characters use textured, modeled human faces and eyes, skinned hair/clothing and a common full-body rig. Facial identity changes come from baked jaw, nose, cheek, lip and eye morphs as well as hair. Eyelid morphs blink locally. Do not replace these models with production capsules or synchronize facial bones. These are reusable generic human assets, not bespoke AAA digital humans.

Future art should preserve interaction coordinates, character clearances and the shared collision definitions. Add material and prop detail that explains the expedition/library setting; avoid unrelated asset clutter or franchise motifs.
